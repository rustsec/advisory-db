# `Gate::read_dir` entries can open, write, and remove files with only `LIST`, even after revoke

`Gate::read_dir` needs only `Rights::LIST`, but it returned cap-std's `ReadDir`. Each entry held the listed directory's descriptor and had `open`, `open_with` (write, create, truncate), `open_dir`, `metadata`, `remove_file`, and `remove_dir`, all relative to that descriptor and none through the gate. So `LIST` gave every right beneath the directory, nothing was audited, and the entries kept working after `revoke` and `narrow`.

A `Broker`'s worker is not affected, because the broker sends names, not entries. After `lock()`, the kernel refuses what the Landlock rules do not give. Code that holds a `Gate` before `lock()` could get around its rights and revocations.

The flaw was corrected in 0.5.0: `Gate::read_dir` returns `Vec<DirEntry>`, with a name and a `FileType` and no descriptor, and `fsgate::ReadDir` is removed. This is a breaking change; replace `entry.open()` with `gate.open(dir.join(entry.file_name()))`, which needs `READ` and is audited.
