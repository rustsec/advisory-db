# MutualTlsManager::authenticate_client accepts forged client certificates (mTLS client authentication bypass)

`MutualTlsManager::authenticate_client` (module `server::security::mtls`, part of the default build) decides whether a PKI client certificate is valid by comparing the certificate's issuer distinguished name with the subject DN of a trusted CA. It never verifies the certificate's signature. A certificate that an attacker creates and signs with their own key, but whose issuer name is copied from a trusted CA, is reported as valid (`MutualTlsAuthResult::is_valid == true`). The same method also skips the CA check entirely when the manager's CA store is empty, so any certificate within its validity period is reported valid.

Applications that call `authenticate_client` and trust `MutualTlsAuthResult::is_valid` to authenticate an OAuth 2.0 client by TLS client certificate (RFC 8705 `tls_client_auth`) can be made to accept a certificate not issued by their trusted CA. The crate's own `FapiManager` does not use `authenticate_client` and was not bypassable through that path.

Affected: 0.4.0 to 0.4.5, 0.5.0-rc1 up to 0.5.0-rc27, and 0.6.0-rc41 (every published version containing `src/server/security/mtls.rs`; 0.1.1, 0.2.0 and 0.3.0 do not contain it). Fixed in 0.4.6, 0.5.0-rc31 and 0.6.0-rc42. Certificate revocation (CRL/OCSP) is not implemented before or after the fix. Workaround: terminate TLS with a verifier that validates the client certificate chain and use this crate only to map the verified certificate to a client.
