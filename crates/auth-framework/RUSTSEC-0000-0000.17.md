# Client address taken from client-controlled forwarded-address headers (rate limiter and authorization context)

Two components trusted `X-Forwarded-For` / `X-Real-IP` (and a `Via` header), which any client can set.

1. **Rate limiter** (`rate_limit_middleware_with_state`, feature `api-server`) keyed requests on the first `X-Forwarded-For` entry. A client could rotate the header to obtain a new bucket per request, and requests without either header shared one global bucket, so one client could exhaust it for everyone.
2. **Authorization context** (`authorization_enhanced`, library-level; the shipped `ApiServer` does not mount its middleware): the client address came from the same headers, and the connection type was chosen partly from a client-supplied `Via` header, so policies keyed on client address or on a "corporate network" could be influenced by the client.

Impact: per-IP throttling of login and other endpoints could be bypassed (credential stuffing, MFA code guessing); headerless clients could be denied service; address-based policies could be satisfied by a forged header.

Affected: rate limiter 0.5.0-rc18 up to 0.6.0-rc45 (no limiter in 0.4.x and earlier); authorization address 0.4.1 to 0.4.6 and 0.5.0-rc1 up to 0.6.0-rc45. Fixed: 0.4.7 (authorization address only) and 0.6.0-rc46 (both). There is no patched release on 0.5.x. Behind a reverse proxy, list its address with `ApiServer::with_trusted_proxies` / `ContextBuilder::with_trusted_proxies`. Workaround: strip these headers at the edge and rate-limit there.
