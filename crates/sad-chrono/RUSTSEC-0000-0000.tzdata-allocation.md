# Excessive memory allocation when loading Android/OpenHarmony `tzdata`

On Android and OpenHarmony, `Local` reads time zone data from the system `tzdata` bundle. The
loader used 32-bit offsets and lengths from the bundle header and index entries to size buffers
without an independent allocation cap. A malformed bundle could request nearly 4 GiB for the index buffer or
for a selected zone entry. Allocation failure could terminate the process, and on 32-bit targets an
excessive request could also panic with a capacity overflow.

`sad-chrono` 0.4.46 did not bound these sizes. `sad-chrono` 0.5.0 checked the requested regions
against the file's logical length, but a large logical file, including a sparse file, could still
cause excessive allocation.

In `sad-chrono` 0.4.47 and 0.5.1, the loader also rejects index regions and selected entries larger
than 1 MiB before allocating their buffers. Separately, 0.4.46 could read a padded bundle's index
from the wrong position; 0.4.47 and 0.5.0 honor the index position declared in the header.

Exposure requires influence over the contents of the bundle the process opens. Android searches
beneath the directories named by `ANDROID_TZDATA_ROOT`, `ANDROID_DATA`, and `ANDROID_ROOT`, in that
order; OpenHarmony uses `/system/etc/zoneinfo/tzdata`.
