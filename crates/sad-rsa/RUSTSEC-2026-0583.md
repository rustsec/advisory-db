# Panics during RSA private-key construction, validation, or re-encoding

`RsaPrivateKey` private-key construction, validation, and serialization can panic across three
classes of inputs, as well as when re-encoding certain valid keys:

- `RsaPrivateKey::from_components` and PKCS#1 or PKCS#8 private-key decoding (DER or PEM) can panic
  during validation when a supplied factor equals 1, because `RsaPrivateKey::validate()` let a
  factor of 1 reach a later check that panicked.
- `RsaPrivateKey::from_primes` and `RsaPrivateKey::from_p_q` can panic during construction when a
  supplied factor is 0, 1, or even.
- Prime recovery in `RsaPrivateKey::from_components` can panic when the private exponent is 0.
- For a key, including a valid one, whose first prime occupies more limbs than the second,
  `crt_coefficient`, `to_pkcs1_der`, `to_pkcs8_der`, and `serde` serialization can panic.

An application that imports untrusted private keys or factors, or re-encodes such keys, can lose a
worker, or the whole process when built with `panic = "abort"`. Public-key parsing is not affected.

Fixed in 0.10.4, which rejects factors below 2, even factors, repeated factors, and a zero private
exponent during prime recovery, and computes the CRT coefficient and re-encodes valid two-prime
keys when the first prime occupies more limbs than the second.

If you cannot upgrade to 0.10.4, isolate the entire import, construction, CRT calculation, or
serialization in a separate process and treat an abnormal exit as an operation failure, or use
`catch_unwind` around the entire operation when panics unwind.
